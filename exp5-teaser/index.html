Screen 3 · ~20sThe brief

New screen
  • One job for this screen: tell the learner what is about to happen and why it is worth 2½ minutes. Three matches, the one they clicked highlighted, and the shape of the flow in three numbers.
  • The managers' stories moved into the swipe cards where they are doing something, instead of being read.
  • One selling line per screen. "Not now" sits next to the primary so leaving is never a hunt.

Screen 4 · ~1 minSix real moments

New screen
  • Two moments per externship, each a real task from Project 1 or 2 of that curriculum, with a company chip so the learner knows whose job it is.
  • Every card names the skill and the role that uses it. That is how "learn the right skills" becomes visible instead of claimed. Skills come from each program's metadata.
  • The bar moves on every swipe. A yes adds to that externship; a pass adds nothing. Confidence is built in view.

Screen 5 · ~20sTwo sliders

New screen
  • The lab's slider mechanic, pointed at the two axes these three jobs actually differ on: the numbers you work with, and whether you value what exists or bet on what's next.
  • Each pole is an externship. The bar moves as the thumb moves, so the learner sees the tradeoff before they commit.

Screen 6 · ~30sOne real call

New screen
  • The question comes from whichever externship is leading. Attronica: the run-rate vs peak principle from Priya's day-one briefing. Yinan: what set Carvana's $3.55 price. Mangusta: which of three startups fits the fund's real thesis.
  • Each is a first principle the curriculum teaches on day one, so the factory can pull the next fourteen from the same callouts.
  • Wrong answers teach, and the button unlocks either way. A miss at a paywall must never be a dead end.

Screen 7 · ~20sThe payoff

New screen
  • One clear answer: a best-match card with the externship's own Learn-more content (overview and the three learn-and-accomplish rows), then one big CTA to start the trial there.
  • Then the proof for the skeptic: the deliverables with the company's name on them, the skills a recruiter reads, and the job line. The runner-up stays one tap away.
  • The cliffhanger is the externship's own open question, unanswered. The trial is where it gets answered.
  • If nothing leans (no externship above 40%), the headline says so and the primary becomes Browse the catalog. Honest, and it keeps the flow from forcing a fit.
