Beat 1 · ~20 secondsThe brief, in the company's own voice

  • Priya is real. She's the engagement lead in the actual curriculum ("Priya Tells You About the Engagement" is substep 1). Naming her makes it a job, not a syllabus.
  • The five-project path is drawn from the real module titles, with Project 1 lit. It answers "how long, and toward what" without a paragraph.
  • One selling line per screen, set in the serif with a brand rule. Here it's the thesis of the whole experiment.
  • The exit is always top-left, small, and honest: "Back to projects".

Beat 2 · ~45 secondsFour moments, not eight

  • Same mechanic as the lab (it tested well and it's cheap to answer), but every card is lifted from the curriculum: the NDA, the 8 ratios, the FP&A write-up, Derek's stress-test.
  • Each card names its step in small type at the bottom, so the peek behind the curtain is literal: this is Step 1, this is Step 3.
  • Four cards, not eight. Card five tells the student nothing card three didn't, and the wall is not the place to spend a minute.
  • One answer per card, enforced. A double tap can't skip a card (the bug we fixed in the engine yesterday).

Beat 3 · ~40 secondsOne real call

  • This is the actual peek. Step 2 of Project 1 is "Calculate the 8 Ratios". The student does one, gets graded instantly, and is told in the feedback that the real thing is this on Attronica's statements.
  • Numbers are labelled as the program's worked example, never Attronica's private figures. The final numbers come from the IES reference company in the curriculum.
  • Three picks, one right. The wrong-answer copy teaches rather than scolds, so a miss still feels like a win.
  • The button unlocks on any answer, right or wrong. A wrong answer at a paywall must not become a dead end.
